1. GAME

.1. Playing Time

1.1.1. The manager will not force any player to play. It is not the intention of the Sierra Division to guarantee that all players receive equal playing time.

1.1.2. In the event a player is injured and forced to leave the game, the player may leave the offensive line up without penalty, provided there are no eligible, available players to take his place. The departing players’ turn at bat will be skipped without recording an out, with the provision that this does not apply where the departing player has been ejected by the umpire. In the case of an ejection with no eligible offensive replacement players, an out will be recorded the first time through the order at the ejected players turn in the order.

1.2. Base Runners

1.2.1. A base-runner may have three different types of replacement runners: a courtesy runner, the catcher’s substitute, or a pinch-runner.

1.2.2. Teams may have unlimited courtesy runners.

1.2.3. If the offensive team replaces the runner with the wrong courtesy runner (a player that was not the last out) and the opposing team protests before the next pitch, then the original runner must run and the right to use the courtesy runner for this particular batter will be forfeited for the balance of the game.

1.2.4. If the original runner inadvertently runs for himself and the opposing team protests after the first pitch, the right to use the courtesy runner for this particular batter will be forfeited for the balance of the game.

1.2.5. If the right to use a courtesy runner is forfeited by violating any of the rules above, the offending team will not have the right to use another courtesy runner for an injured player.

1.2.6. In addition to declared courtesy runners, the catcher, should he be on the bases with two outs in the inning, may utilize a courtesy runner. This substitution is optional for the offensive team.

1.2.7. All courtesy runners used, as well as the catcher’s substitute runner, must be the "last out," as defined herein.

1.2.7.1. For the purpose of these rules, "last out" is defined as follows. The last out will be the last player, currently in the lineup, to have been declared out either at the plate or as a on base runner. If no eligible "last out" can be determined from the last four batters to appear at the plate prior to the batter now needing a replacement runner, then the fifth batter prior will become the substitute runner. A player currently on base, or having already declared himself in need of a substitute runner, or a catcher with two outs, obviously is not eligible to become the replacement runner.

1.2.7.2 A batter must touch 1st base (or 2nd or 3rd base if there is an extra base hit) and "time" must be granted before he is replaced on the base.

1.2.7.3 Courtesy runners are ineligible to steal bases. Courtesy runners may advance when the ball crosses the plate, wild pitch/passed balls, or when the ball is put into play.

1.2.8. If a pinch-runner is used, the pinch-runner must be out of the lineup at that time, and must assume the position in the lineup of the player for whom he is running for the next time through the batting order.

1.3. Forfeits

1.3.1. In the event of two forfeits in a single season, the status of the team and the general manager will be reviewed by the Commissioner, and reported to all other GMs. Possible ramifications include asking the team’s GM to resign, the team to be prohibited from registering for the following season, or the team to be barred from the playoffs. This will be determined by a vote of all Divisional GMs.

1.3.2. There are no limitations to the number of players a team may carry on a roster, and plenty of taxi-pool players are available. Thus, there is no reason to ever forfeit. However, if circumstances are deemed extraordinary, a team’s GM may appeal the penalties in the league wide rules to the Commissioner. This appeal is only possible for a first forfeit.

1.4. Dugout Selection

1.4.1 The home team has the right to choose which dugout or bench it will occupy for the game.

1.5. Wood Bats

1.5.1. Only "all wood" bats and "composite-wood" bats (as defined in PSSBL rule 1.3.1) are allowed.

2. Rosters

2.1. Buddy Picks

2.1.1. Each team shall be awarded “unlimited” buddy picks.

2.1.2 Buddy picks may be added by a GM prior to the team’s 12th game of the season. If game play has already begun, the GM should obtain the consent of his team prior to adding a player to the roster in this way.

2.2. The Draft

2.2.1. Draft order is determined by final standings, defined as the overall W-L record combining the regular season and playoff games. Teams draft in the reverse order of their final standing, from bottom to top. Teams that are new to the division will be added to the end of each round (see New Teams section.) There will be as many rounds as needed until no team is willing to draft a player.

2.2.2. It is not necessary to draft a player if a team’s roster is sufficiently ample to meet the demands of the forthcoming season. However, each team should participate in the try-out process by offering representatives to assist the League’s Player Agent and President on try-out days.

2.3. Immediate Family

2.3.1. A family member, for the purpose of these rules, is strictly defined as a parent, sibling, child or spouse.

2.3.2. A team may draft an immediate family member of a player drafted by declaring its intent at the time it drafts the first of the family members, in exchange for its regular third round pick.

2.3.3. A team may add a player to its roster that is an immediate family member of a returning player without expending a Buddy Pick or subjecting the player to the PSSBL draft. These picks must be added to the team before the season’s draft. A team may only use this rule once per season.

2.4 Returning Players

2.4.1. A team may add any player who is returning to the division after more than a one year absence to their roster without using a buddy pick, draft pick or other compensation, PROVIDED the team was the last team who owned that player’s rights. For this rule, a team last owned the rights of a player if the team was the last Sierra team for which the player played AND the player was not either subsequently officially traded or cut. The returning player in this case, since they have sat out over a year, is a free agent and is not required to return to the team that last owned his rights.

3. Taxi-Pool Use

3.1. A GM or manager of a team should never allow his team to forfeit a game through being unable to field a team on-time. The penalties are discussed in the League and Sierra rules, and are severe. The GM or manager has access to the Taxi-Pool of players in order to prevent this from ever being an occurrence.

3.2. Fill in (taxi-pool) players will be obtained through the use of the official PSSBL online taxi pool system on the PSSBL website or by contacting the Player Agent. The Player Agent (or the League President, if the Player Agent cannot be contacted) should be given at least 48 hours notice. The Player Agent and League President will have the list of eligible Taxi Pool players.

3.3. A team must make a new request online or to the Player Agent every time it needs a player. Teams may not contact the same players repeatedly. If you believe a taxi-pool player was obtained illegally, contact the Commissioner and an investigation will be made.

3.4. Taxi-pool players will not be allowed to pitch, unless agreed to by the opposing team’s GM or manager prior to the start of the game.

3.5. Teams may not call anyone, other than those on his Taxi-Pool list, to play. An exception will be made if you have not received a response via the online taxi pool tool AND you called the Player Agent at least 48 hours in advance of the game and you were not able to get any taxi pool players to turn out. You must call the Commissioner before the game and get a verbal OK to call a player known to you as well as notify the opposing manager. The contacted player may only play if they are the 8th or 9th player.

3.6. Using an ineligible player will result in your team forfeiting that game, at minimum.

3.7. All taxi-pool players utilized must be at least 35 years of age within the calendar year, and have registered for the PSSBL Taxi Pool, and paid the fee.

3.8. All taxi pool players asked to participate in a game will receive at least three innings of defensive play and will be in the lineup for the entire course of the game, with the exception of injury.

3.9. GMs must report the names of taxi pool players on the lineup card at the beginning of the game.

3.10. A team using a taxi-pool player must bat every available roster player in the batting lineup for the duration of the game, including, of course, the taxi-pool player, with the exception of injury. All taxi pool players are to be added at the end of the lineup.

3.11. Taxi-pool players are not eligible for the playoffs.

4. Trades

4.1. A player may be traded with the consent of the player. The division will attempt to accommodate player requests to change teams, so long as the change is not initiated by one of the other existing teams, and so long as the team losing the player is adequately compensated.

4.2. Trades must be approved by all players involved, and both GMs. As well, the Commissioner may defer approval of a trade to determine if it is beneficial to all parties involved, including the Division.

4.3. All trades must be filed with the PSSBL Player Agent on the proper form in order to be official.

4.4. Draft choices are allowed as compensation in trades.

5. Cuts

5.1. Cuts can be made at the discretion of the GM, keeping in my mind the best interests of the team and the player.

5.2. Cuts should only be made between the end of the season (after the championship game) and the League cut deadline. Any circumstances that warrant cutting a player during the season need to be discussed and approved by the Commissioner.

6. New Teams

6.1 A new team to the Division must first gain the approval of the Commissioner, and of the League Board.

6.2. A new team can have a multitude of meanings: a hybrid of several disbanded teams, the nucleus of a team from another PSSBL Division, or a team with a nucleus of players who are from outside the PSSBL.

7. Post-Season

7.1. Playoffs

7.1.1. Playoff seeds are determined by order of regular season finish.

7.1.2. Playoff format will be as follows:

7.2.2.1. First Round: #4 through #9 seeds play a one game playoff. #1 through #3 seeds get a bye.

7.2.2.2. Second round: #3 seed plays lowest seed to advance, remaining games are matched up by seed and are all best of 3. #1 and #2 seeds get a bye.

7.2.2.3. Third round: Winners of previous round play #1 and #2 seeds. #1 seed plays lowest seed to advance. Best of 3.

7.2.2.4. Finals: Remaining teams play in a best of three championship series.

7.2.2.5. Home team (in first and third games) in all playoff series is the highest seeded team.

7.1.3. If two, or more, teams finish the season with identical regular-season records, the following tiebreakers apply (in order) to determine playoff seeding:

a. Results of head-to-head competition, (not total runs scored, just wins).

b. A point system that provides more points for victories over higher finishing teams. Example: In a 10-team division, victories against the first place team are worth 10 points while victories against the 10th place team are worth one point. The higher point total earns the higher seed.

c. The same point system applied in reverse for losses within the division. Example: In a 10-team division, loses to the first place team are worth 1 point while loses to the 10th place team are worth 10 points. The lower point total earns the higher seed.

d. Coin toss.

7.2 Playoff eligibility

7.2.1 An eligible player, on a team prior to season start must have played in 25% of his teams regular season games or, if a player is added to his team’s roster by the eligibility date (July 15) said player must meet the requirement of having played in 25% regular season games for which they are eligible.

8. All-Star Game

8.1. Should an All-Star game be organized by the League, each team will elect three players to participate in the game, as well as three alternates. The game is normally 4th of July Weekend. At the discretion of the Commissioner, in order to field a complete team, the number of participants may be increased.

9. Other Tournaments

9.1. All players are encouraged, as they see fit and are able, to form teams to participate in Regional and/or National tournaments. The GMs and Commissioner are willing to offer assistance in supporting the organization of such Tournament teams.
